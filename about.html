About Wordbridge AAC

The same facts the app carries on its own About screen, written out. The people deciding whether to trust this with a child are owed all of it in one place.

Who made it

Wordbridge AAC is developed by Shey Al and Haley Dalrymple. The commit history carries a handle; a caregiver deciding whether to trust this with a child is owed names.

How it was built

This app was developed with the assistance of generative AI.

Said plainly, and not softened by anything around it. A caregiver, a teacher or a therapist is entitled to know how the thing was built before they decide what it is allowed to do, and a disclaimer they have to go looking for is one they will not find. It is on the About screen inside the app for the same reason.

License

The app is free and open source under the MIT license. The source is on GitHub, so what the app does can be read rather than taken on trust.

Core vocabulary

The words the board starts with are Universal Core 36, by the Center for Literacy and Disability Studies, University of North Carolina. Everything beyond that first set is drawn from published vocabulary research, and where it is judgment rather than research the derivation is written down in the repository.

The board's layout

The colors and the left to right sentence order are the Modified Fitzgerald Key, after Edith Fitzgerald, and they are not this project's invention. The scheme descends from one Fitzgerald built for deaf education in the 1920s, and most of the AAC field has used a version of it since.

The pictures

The pictures that ship with the app come from four sets, all under CC BY SA 4.0 and reached through Global Symbols: Mulberry Symbols, Stellar Symbols, Tawasol, and OpenMoji. The app can also draw on the emoji already on the device.

ARASAAC is available as well, switched on by whoever wants it rather than bundled, because its license does not permit commercial use and that restriction belongs to the person who chose it rather than to everyone who installs the app.

Every set's full credit line, and the credit for any single picture, is inside the app under Settings, then About, then Symbol credits.

The version you have

The app shows its version and build on its own About screen, under Settings. Quoting it in an email to support makes an answer faster.
